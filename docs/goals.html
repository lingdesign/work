01Context

What Fay does, and what was missing

Fay connects patients with insurance-covered registered dietitians across 30+ specialties, from weight management to diabetes to eating disorders. For most patients, results don’t come from the appointment itself. They come from what happens after it, day to day: the small goals a dietitian prescribes and a patient practices between sessions.

Dietitians were already prescribing these goals in every session, as part of a real care plan. The app wasn’t part of that plan, and something else had taken its place.

02Before

The old reward loop: patients chased a chance to win, not their health

Goals in the app worked like a game. Every patient started with the same three default goals, modeled on another gamification app, and checked them off to enter a prize draw. Engagement looked healthy, but it was engagement with the gambling, not with care.

Dietitians couldn’t see any progress, and the goals they wrote in their notes never appeared in the app.

03Dietitian side

Dietitians needed help writing goals, not just sending them

Sending goals from the chart note to the patient’s app was the easy part. The hard part was the goals themselves: written for a chart, they read as clinical, vague or long. A patient needs a small, practical action they can do today.

I prototyped two ways for AI to help dietitians write them, built with Claude, so the PM and engineers could react to working versions, not static mockups: an inline rewrite that suggests a patient-friendly version as the dietitian types, and a rewrite for the whole list at once. Either way, the dietitian reviews and confirms before anything reaches the patient.

04Onboarding

Every patient starts with goals for their condition, not the same three

Here is what a patient who comes in for emotional eating sees. Onboarding asks what they want support with, then puts together a few goals as starting points, to tailor with their dietitian in the appointment.

I designed the goal library to support the different health conditions our patients come in with. These are small daily tools to complement the dietitian goals.

05Motion design

The loading moment sets the pace: small steps, over time

Just before this screen, a patient has named what they’ve been struggling with: emotional eating, gut health, an eating disorder, or the moment they finally decided to work on their weight. From my research, I knew many had already tried a lot that didn’t work. So we designed the wait for that moment: calm, and building confidence, with “One small step today. A healthier you over time.” The next screen gives them small steps they can start right away.

Putting the goals together takes a moment, so we made the wait part of the message. I directed the animation with our Rive motion contractor Akash, and my design colleague Tori turned my direction into a storyboard spec. I then worked with our mobile engineers to build it into our onboarding.

06Building it

I saved build time by finding where the cost really was

The completion animation was at risk of getting cut because of a tight deadline. On a call with our iOS and Android engineers, I looked for where the cost actually was. It wasn’t the animation: it was playing it inside the native bottom sheet. So I moved the celebration to an overlay above the sheet: one day to build, in time for our release cut.

Repeat settings went the same way. Dietitian goal data showed most goals were daily, some weekly and a few one-time, and a custom time picker had confused people in testing. Quick prototypes with engineering led us to each platform’s native menu, with the differences between iOS and Android documented: nothing new for patients to learn, and a week of build became a day.

07Outcome

Patients now practice their dietitian’s goals, and dietitians can see how it’s going

Patients used to chase a prize draw. Now they practice the goals their dietitian set in the appointment, and track their progress over time.

Dietitians see that progress since the last appointment, so they know how a patient is doing and can adjust the plan together. Goals that count for something, and goals a dietitian manages with the patient: a stronger care relationship between appointments.
